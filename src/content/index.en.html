Proposal A citizen-facing eGov service for Kazakhstan

Kuä bol. See, check and correct what the state records about you.

Kuä is a proposed eGov service. It would show citizens every access to their records, find contradictions between state registries, and help them get errors corrected by the agencies responsible for them.

Try the demo {{icon:arrow}}Read the analysis

{{icon:info}}Kuä (Куә) means “witness” in Kazakh, so “Kuä bol” means “be a witness”.

{{hero}}

Why it matters

Kazakhstan ranks 16th in the world for digital government. Its records still disagree.

eGov does not produce the data it displays. It shows records from separate state registries, and only the agency responsible for a record can correct it. Meanwhile, the same people's data keeps leaking from different organisations.

{{stats}}

How it works

Four functions

AI is used only for explaining, identifying the responsible agency and drafting. It does not edit records or make decisions.

Full design {{icon:arrow}}
01 · VIEW

See every access to your records

A single list of every access to and change in a citizen's records, by state bodies and companies, in Kazakh, Russian or English.

  • Access by law enforcement is included. Where details are restricted, Kuä still records that “an exempt body accessed [category] on [date]” and tells the citizen where to request details.
  • Access by staff also appears in the log, so misuse by insiders becomes visible.
No AIStrict approach
{{loop:view}}
02 · CHECK

Find records that contradict each other

Standard software, not AI, compares a citizen's records across registries and flags contradictions.

  • Using standard software for comparisons also keeps energy use down.
  • Every mismatch is recorded, anonymised and by registry, for the public scorecard.
Rule-based · no AI
{{loop:check}}
03 · CORRECT

Get errors fixed by the agency that made them

AI explains each mismatch, identifies the agency responsible for the record and drafts a correction request or complaint. The citizen decides whether to send it.

  • The original record is always shown next to the AI's explanation, because language models perform worse in Kazakh than in English.
  • Correction stays a human process with legal deadlines.
AI draftsYou decide
{{loop:correct}}
04 · ESCALATE

Unanswered requests leave the ministry

Requests that receive no response are passed automatically to oversight bodies outside the ministry.

  • Unresolved cases go to the Human Rights Commissioner.
  • Every case is counted in a public scorecard, and the Supreme Audit Chamber reviews the service each year.
Automatic rulesOutside the ministry

Governance

State-run, checked from outside

Kuä needs access to state registries, so it has to be a state service. But the personal-data regulator, the Committee on Information Security, belongs to the same ministry that runs eGov. A service that only the ministry can check would leave the operator supervising itself.

The proposal therefore takes a middle option: the state operates Kuä, but oversight is shared among several bodies outside the ministry.

Inclusion

Built for people who are often left out

Accessibility is a design principle, not an add-on. Rural citizens and people with disabilities are also the groups that current data leaves out.

{{icon:mic}}

Voice input

Speak instead of typing.

{{icon:text}}

Plain-language mode

Short sentences, with no legal or technical terms.

{{icon:ear}}

Screen readers and large text

Every screen works with screen readers and can be enlarged.

{{icon:users}}

A trusted helper

A relative can help with the citizen's consent, since many older and disabled people handle paperwork with family.

{{icon:store}}

Always available offline

All functions are also available at a Public Service Centre (ЦОН), in line with the AI Law's right to refuse interaction with AI.

{{icon:globe}}

Three languages

Kazakh, Russian and English, with accuracy published separately for each.
