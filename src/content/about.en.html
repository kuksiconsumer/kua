The project

This site is the final project for PLS 419: AI from a Social Science Perspective at Nazarbayev University (Fall 2026). The assignment asks for an AI-assisted answer to a real problem in Kazakhstan, together with an analysis of its costs.

Kuä is a proposal, not a service. It does not connect to eGov, collect personal data or run live AI. The demo on the home page is scripted, and every person, address and date in it is fictional.

AI transparency statement

This project was developed with Claude (Anthropic) within my PLS419 project.

AI contributed

Searching for and verifying sources and DOIs, reading and summarising the papers I uploaded, the risk analysis, the cost calculations, draft text for all four sections, and the code, translations and illustrations for this website.

I decided

  • the topic, based on my own experience of mismatched records;
  • to change the original idea, an “America.gov for Kazakhstan”, into a service that lets citizens check their own records;
  • the name and slogan;
  • to make it an official service with independent oversight;
  • the strict approach to recording access by security services;
  • to use AlemGPT;
  • to build in accessibility;
  • the website's structure, languages, themes, fonts and illustration style.

I rejected

  • copying America.gov's design;
  • including fact-checking, which is a separate problem;
  • a fully independent platform, which would not have access to state data.

Other AI tools

I used an AI search summary at an early stage. I replaced its claims with primary sources and removed those I could not verify, including Instagram sources.

Verification

Every figure on this site was checked against the sources listed on the Sources page.

Translations

The original version of this site is in English. The Kazakh and Russian versions are raw drafts and will be double-checked. Where a translation differs from the English text, the English text is the reference.

Names of laws, institutions and sources follow their usual form in each language. Quotations from sources are translated, not quoted directly, in the Kazakh and Russian versions.

Design and accessibility

  • Layout: the clear, minimal structure takes inspiration from America.gov, with its own fonts, colours and illustrations.
  • Type: headings in Literata and body text in Inter, both self-hosted and both covering Kazakh Cyrillic letters (ә, ғ, қ, ң, ө, ұ, ү, һ, і).
  • Themes: system, light (pastel blue) and dark (dark blue), remembered on your device.
  • Motion: every looping animation has a pause button, and all motion stops if your device is set to reduce motion.
  • Charts: each chart has a text table under “Show the data as a table” for screen readers.
  • Privacy: no cookies, analytics or external requests. Language and theme choices are stored only in your browser.
